## 7. ASL phonology from all 543 landmarks, no training (TODO §3.10)

The user asked for a phonology experiment: research ASL's phonological features, convert `(T, 543, 3)`
into phonological features only, normalize to a fixed point with and without shoulder-width scaling,
and measure within- vs between-gloss similarity with tolerance. Built `sb.recognize.phonology` (130
features per frame, 18 discrete codes) and `sb.recognize.phonology_stats`; notebook
`gislr.0.dataset.phonology-features.ipynb`, run on all 94,477 clips.

- 41.4% nearest-template top-1 and 54.3% 1-NN (test.csv), vs 3.1% / 13.0% for the raw 543 landmarks
  and 15.1% / 22.2% for raw hands + pose. Handshape alone 25.8%.
- Scaled and unscaled agree within 0.6 points everywhere (shoulder width CV 0.11 on GISLR).
- Same-gloss clips by different signers are half as similar as by the same signer; per-signer accuracy
  17–59%.
- Tolerance works on the combination, not per feature: 9 informative codes match exactly for 5.4% of
  same-gloss pairs vs 0.3% of different-gloss pairs.
- Data findings, checked against Google's original parquet: both hands appear together in 0.27% of
  GISLR frames; the non-dominant arm is raised in 13% of clips, as often for two-handed signs as
  one-handed ones.

Report: `docs/reports/asl-phonology-features.md`.
