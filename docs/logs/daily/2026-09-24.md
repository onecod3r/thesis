## 5. Sign → speech: the downstream stages (TODO §12.6)

- **Built:**
  - `sb.rescore.prior` (Kneser-Ney n-grams, held-out folds, intrinsic metrics) and a GRU LM;
  - `sb.recognize.continuous.fuse` (the recognizer vote fused with the prior; rules
    `none`/`rescore`/`agree` + a `max_len` noise gate). With no prior it reproduces C1 D3
    exactly: 0.29338 on all 5,054 streams;
  - `sb.recognize.sequences.noise` (fidget/hold/reverse blocks);
  - `sb.rescore.gloss2en`, the reverse of rules_v2;
  - Workers AI LLM and TTS clients; a draft 132-sentence English reference set;
  - `sb.recognize.continuous.online.OnlineDecoder` (the client's frame-by-frame loop, 0/2,400
    mismatches vs batch);
  - four notebooks.
- **Measured:**
  - The trigram predicts the next gloss top-5 30% of the time on unseen sentences (27.5%
    on unseen topics). The GRU LM, trained in the user's run of the notebook, ranks worse (25%),
    so the n-gram ships.
  - Fused gently (λ=0.3) it lowers selection GER **0.413 → 0.401**. The user's `agree`
    rule trades wrong signs for missing ones (up to GER 0.730), so it is a precision mode.
    The demo's first default used it and scored 0.598, which is how this was found.
  - Rule-based gloss → English: BLEU 60.2 vs 7.9 for the raw glosses.
  - Windows OS voices (≈ the browser's `speechSynthesis`) are intelligible to Whisper at
    WER 0–0.3% in 0.2 s. Aura-2 costs about 120 neurons per sentence, about 80/day on
    Free, so **speech defaults to the browser**.
  - **End to end on 300 held-out streams, the browser's TFLite model + `OnlineDecoder`
    equal the offline path (293 identical, 7 float near-ties, 0 unexplained): GER 0.245,
    47% of sentences exact.**

## 6. Floor-recall experiment (evening)

- The user asked for fewer missed signs under the confidence floor, without more wrong or
  extra signs. Claude diagnosed it, planned it and, after approval, built `sb.recognize.continuous.select`
  (alternative scores, per-sign floors, a fixed-lag lattice, a logistic accept/reject) and
  `gislr.4.downstream.acceptance.ipynb`.
- **The user's run:** the θ grid was too coarse for the budget, so every method was chosen
  with more misses than the floor. Claude added a θ refinement step (§5b) and ran it on the
  selection signers.
- **Result:** waiting one sign before deciding (lattice lag 1) misses **2.4 fewer signs per 100
  on clean sentences and 1.6 fewer on noisy** at the floor's wrong/extra/noise rates, for about
  20 frames of delay. Noise remains the ceiling. The evaluation-signer check is the user's
  next run.
  → [reports/sign-to-speech-downstream.md](../../reports/sign-to-speech-downstream.md) §2.2

## 7. Demo diagnosis (late evening)

- The user reported that the sign → speech demo "outputs very misleading glosses and the
  translation is way off". The analysis covered all 5,054 held-out streams:
  - the committed demo ran with no floor, so 48% of sentences contain a wrong or invented gloss;
  - the printed confidence would have flagged most of them (≤0.2 → 27% right);
  - 90% of wrong glosses are unrelated signs, not look-alike pairs;
  - the rule engine makes fluent English out of anything, and on true glosses it still fails
    on noun lists, locatives, clauses and `finish`.
- Suggested fixes, ranked: pin the floor/lattice, show uncertainty, LLM with top-3
  alternatives, rule patches, end-to-end English metric, better recognizer, real-signing test.
  → [reports/sign-to-speech-demo-analysis.md](../../reports/sign-to-speech-demo-analysis.md)
